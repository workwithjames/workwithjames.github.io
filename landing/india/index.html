Direct answer

Can an Indian resident buy property in Dubai?

Yes. RBI guidance says resident individuals may use the Liberalised Remittance Scheme to purchase property abroad, subject to the scheme limit and authorised-dealer procedures. The current LRS limit is USD 250,000 per resident individual per financial year. India's tax authority lists TCS rules for overseas remittances, including a ₹10 lakh threshold and 20% rate for many 'other purpose' remittances above it as of May 2026. Apply the current rules with your bank and Indian tax adviser before transferring funds.

  • Confirm each remitter's available LRS limit
  • Ask the bank for purpose code and documents
  • Plan TCS cash flow and tax-credit timing
  • Verify Dubai title, fees and payment recipient

India-specific framework

What Indian buyers should solve before selecting a Dubai unit

Ownership access is only the first question. Funding, documentation, Indian reporting and the unit's economics need equal attention.

RBI LRS availability

RBI's current FAQ states USD 250,000 per resident individual per financial year for permitted transactions. Check used limits, joint ownership and remitter-to-buyer documentation.

Bank execution

An authorised dealer may request PAN, declarations, source-of-funds, property and beneficiary documents. Build lead time into booking and instalment dates.

TCS cash-flow effect

TCS can create a temporary cash requirement even when ultimately available as credit subject to tax rules. Confirm rate, threshold and credit treatment for your remittance.

INR/AED movement

The AED is US-dollar pegged, so an INR-funded buyer carries INR/USD-linked movement. Use a contingency rather than assuming today's conversion for future instalments.

India tax and disclosure

Foreign rental income, gains, assets or accounts can create Indian tax-return and disclosure questions depending on residence and ownership. Obtain personalised advice.

Residency is not automatic

Property ownership and UAE residence are separate. Current official Golden Visa guidance lists a five-year real-estate route from AED 2 million, subject to conditions.

Indian investor FAQs

Buying Dubai property from India

RBI and tax rules can change. Confirm the current position with the authorised dealer and qualified Indian adviser for each transfer.

Can an Indian resident use LRS to buy Dubai property?

Yes. RBI's overseas property FAQ states resident individuals can use LRS for property abroad. The transaction must follow current limits, purpose and authorised-dealer procedures.

What is the current LRS limit?

RBI's current FAQ states USD 250,000 per resident individual per financial year for permitted current or capital account transactions, or a combination. Used limits and buyer-remitter documentation matter.

How does TCS affect a Dubai property payment?

India's tax authority lists a ₹10 lakh threshold and 20% TCS for many LRS remittances for 'other purposes' as of May 2026. TCS is a tax collection and potential credit mechanism, but timing and applicability require bank and tax advice.

Can family members combine LRS limits?

Multiple resident individuals may each use available LRS limits, but ownership, remittance purpose, documentation and beneficial interest must be genuine and accepted by the authorised dealer. Do not assume aggregation without advice.

Should I buy off-plan or ready property?

Off-plan can spread payments but introduces construction, completion and contract risk. Ready property enables inspection and rent evidence but usually concentrates cash near transfer. Compare the total INR/AED timeline.

Can Indian buyers own freehold property in Dubai?

Foreign nationals can own eligible property in designated Dubai freehold areas. Confirm the exact project's title type and registration documents.

Will Dubai property qualify me for a UAE visa?

Not automatically. The UAE government's current guidance describes a five-year real-estate investor Golden Visa from AED 2 million, subject to eligibility conditions and authority approval.
