How they differ

Four different buying decisions, not four versions of the same package.

The difference is the level of uncertainty being removed and the amount of execution or ownership required.

Diagnostic

Find what is wrong

Use it to identify the highest-priority commercial and digital gaps before spending on a larger fix.

Sprint

Decide what to do

Use it when the business needs a structured decision, roadmap and implementation direction.

Project

Build or fix the system

Use it when the required outcome is clear enough to move into design, development or implementation.

Partnership

Operate and improve it

Use it when performance needs recurring optimisation, coordination and senior commercial attention.

Fractional leadership sits above these models when the need is not one project, but ongoing direction across teams, agencies, suppliers and priorities.

Before you approve a quote

The commercial basics should already be clear.

The proposal is where the exact commercial commitment is locked, but the buying logic should not be hidden until that point.

Scope

What you receive

Separate costs

Pricing FAQ

Questions a buyer should not have to chase for an answer.

If your situation does not fit a standard entry point, the first conversation is used to identify the smallest sensible scope.

Are the published prices fixed quotes?

No. They are transparent starting points. Your proposal confirms the exact deliverables, timing, review rounds, responsibilities and fee before work starts.

Why not publish a maximum price or fixed package?

The same label can hide very different levels of complexity. A single landing system is not commercially equivalent to a multilingual website, a CRM integration or a multi-market programme. You receive the actual quote before committing.

Is advertising spend included in the $5K/month Growth Partnership?

No unless the proposal explicitly says otherwise. Media budget is normally separate, as are relevant software, data services, licences and specialist third-party production.

What is normally covered in the Website / Conversion System?

Typical scope can include messaging and conversion architecture, responsive interface design, front-end development, forms and CTA journeys, technical SEO foundations, analytics and event tracking. The final scope is confirmed before kickoff.

Does the website price include unlimited pages, copy or integrations?

No. Page count is not the pricing model. Full copy production, advanced integrations, multilingual requirements, ecommerce and other specialist needs are scoped according to the commercial journey and implementation effort.

How many revision rounds are included?

The review process and number of rounds are stated in the written proposal. This avoids vague unlimited-revision promises and makes responsibilities clear before production begins.

How do deposits and payment schedules work?

Payment timing, deposits and invoicing are defined for the specific engagement in the accepted proposal or agreement. You see those terms before the project begins.

Who owns the website, source files and accounts?

Ownership, licences, reusable components, source files and handover rights are defined per engagement. Client-owned accounts and third-party assets remain subject to the relevant account and provider terms.

What happens if the scope changes after kickoff?

Work outside the agreed scope can require a change request, revised fee or revised timing. The change should be agreed before additional work is treated as part of the project.

What if I do not know which engagement I need?

Start with the business problem, current constraints and desired outcome. If the right implementation is not yet clear, a smaller diagnostic can be used before a larger commitment.
