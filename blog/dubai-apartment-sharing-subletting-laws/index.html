Dubai apartment sharing and subletting laws: What tenants and owners need to know

Apartment sharing is not banned in Dubai, but informal subletting, overcrowded bed spaces, and unapproved partitions can expose tenants and owners to legal, safety, and financial consequences.

By James RaviPublished 1 August 20268 min read
In this guideShort answerSublettingRoommates2026 lawPermits and safetyPenaltiesChecklistFAQ

Quick answer

Can you share or sublet an apartment in Dubai?

You cannot informally rent out a room, bed, or part of your apartment. Under the existing Dubai tenancy law, subletting or allowing a third party to use the property generally requires the landlord's written consent, unless the tenancy contract states otherwise. Apartment sharing may be possible only when the arrangement complies with the contract, official registration, occupancy, building, and safety requirements.

Important timing: Dubai Law No. 4 of 2026 has been issued and creates a new permit-based framework for shared housing. The official Dubai Media Office announcement states that it takes effect 180 days after publication in the Official Gazette. Until then, existing tenancy, building, safety, and occupancy rules remain relevant.

What counts as subletting in Dubai?

Subletting happens when the named tenant gives another person the right to use all or part of the rented property, usually in exchange for payment. Renting a bedroom, partition, or bed space can therefore be treated as subletting, even when the main tenant continues to live in the apartment.

Article 24 of Dubai Law No. 26 of 2007 says a tenant may not assign the use of or sublease a property to a third party without the landlord's written consent, unless the parties have agreed otherwise. Article 25 allows a landlord to seek eviction where a tenant sublets all or part of a property without written approval. The eviction can extend to the subtenant.

Before accepting payment from another occupant

  • Read the tenancy contract for any clause covering additional occupants or subletting.
  • Obtain the landlord's approval in writing. A verbal agreement is difficult to prove.
  • Check the building's community and occupancy rules.
  • Do not create partitions or alter the unit without the required approvals.

Are roommates allowed in Dubai?

Dubai has not imposed a general ban on roommates. The legality depends on how the arrangement is structured and whether it satisfies the tenancy contract and official requirements. For ordinary visitors rather than paying occupants, read the Dubai tenant guest-access guide.

Lower-risk arrangements include family members living with the named tenant, approved occupants recorded through the correct channels, and residents living in a unit lawfully designated and permitted for shared housing. Informal bed spaces, unapproved room rentals, overcrowding, and unauthorised partitions are not treated the same way.

What changes under Dubai Law No. 4 of 2026?

The new law establishes a dedicated framework for units used as shared housing. It covers authorised owners, residents of designated shared-housing units, and licensed establishments that manage or lease units for shared accommodation. Collective labour accommodation is excluded from this framework.

According to the official Dubai Media Office summary, the law is intended to protect owners and residents, prevent overcrowding and informal housing, improve health and safety, address building and land-use violations, and support fair rental practices.

Who may lease a shared-housing unit?

Under the new framework, only the property owner or an authorised establishment may lease a unit designated for shared housing. A tenant or another party may not sublease any part of that unit. Permitted structures include direct leasing by the owner, management by a licensed establishment, or an authorised company leasing from the owner and subleasing to residents.

What permits and standards apply?

A unit cannot be allocated for shared housing under the new framework without a permit. Dubai Municipality will oversee permits through a unified digital platform, while the Dubai Land Department will maintain the electronic shared-housing registry and specify contract data.

The official announcement says permits are normally valid for one year. An owner may request a two-year permit, and renewal applications must be filed at least 30 days before expiry.

A permitted unit must address

  • Building and technical standards
  • Maximum occupancy and minimum space per resident
  • Adequate shared facilities
  • Health, fire, sanitation, security, and electrical requirements
  • Accurate resident and contract records
  • Rules governing advertising and promotion

What are the penalties for non-compliance?

The official Dubai announcement states that violations may attract fines from AED 500 to AED 500,000. If the same violation is repeated within one year, the fine may be doubled up to AED 1 million.

Additional measures may include suspension of the activity for up to six months, cancellation of the permit, revocation of the commercial licence, disconnection of public services until the violation is corrected, or eviction of units that do not meet permit requirements. The Rental Disputes Center has jurisdiction over disputes arising from the new framework.

Practical checklist for tenants and owners

For tenants

  1. Read the Ejari and tenancy contract.
  2. Identify whether money changes hands for a room or bed.
  3. Request written landlord consent before any subletting arrangement.
  4. Verify building, occupancy, and registration rules.
  5. Avoid unapproved partitions and overcrowding.

For owners and operators

  1. Confirm whether the unit is eligible for shared housing.
  2. Obtain the required permit before allocating the unit.
  3. Use an authorised leasing or management structure.
  4. Meet occupancy, facility, health, and safety standards.
  5. Keep contracts and resident records updated.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tenant sublet an apartment in Dubai?

Only with the landlord's written consent, unless the tenancy contract provides otherwise. Unauthorised subletting can support an eviction claim under Dubai tenancy law.

Is apartment sharing banned in Dubai?

No. Sharing is not generally banned, but the arrangement must comply with contractual, registration, occupancy, building, safety, and permit requirements.

Is written landlord approval enough?

It is essential for subletting under the existing tenancy framework, but it may not be the only requirement. Building rules, occupant registration, safety standards, land-use controls, and shared-housing permits may also apply.

Can a tenant advertise a bed space?

Informal bed-space advertising is high risk. The new framework regulates how shared-housing units are promoted and limits leasing to owners or authorised establishments.

When does Dubai Law No. 4 of 2026 take effect?

The official announcement says the law takes effect 180 days after its publication in the Official Gazette.
